# Trash Panda Aerospace

A TypeScript + HTML5 Canvas + Vite launcher game with playable Earth and Moon progression, illustrated character/scenery assets, synthesized audio, and Canvas menus.

Current flight artwork, eight-frame transitions, anatomical scaling, and browser verification are documented in [the flight animation pass](docs/flight-animation-pass.md). The implementation notes farther below record earlier revisions.

The current [tablet payday, menu/flight music, and fuel changes](docs/payday-music-fuel.md) include the supplied MP3 tracks, animated results, 20% larger tanks, and early Fuel Absorber access.

The [cloud, collection, and salvage pass](docs/sky-collection-salvage.md) adds painted parallax clouds, visible magnet flights, slower per-stat payday equations, and 10-debris satellite top landings.

The [modular artwork HUD and music correction](docs/modular-hud-music.md) uses the supplied desktop/mobile frames, fitted resource bars, an offline radar before purchase, and one exclusive soundtrack player.

The [scrap, deployables, and cold pass](docs/scrap-deployables-cold.md) keeps the flight cash counter strictly to coins plus platforms, uses the supplied scrap/thruster/teleporter art, adds numbered/tappable Deployables slots, and scales frost and health warnings with suit protection.

## Start locally

Requires Node.js 20.19+ or 22.12+.

```sh
npm install
npm run dev
```

Open the local URL printed by Vite (normally http://127.0.0.1:5173). In Windows PowerShell, use `npm.cmd` if the npm PowerShell shim is blocked. `npm run build` checks TypeScript and creates `dist/`; `npm run preview` serves that build. `npm run check` runs a small set of mechanics and persistence checks, not a gameplay simulation.

## Play

1. Select one of three independent slots. Enter an optional pilot name, then choose New Game; occupied slots require an explicit replace action. Continue Game restores that slot's saved progress.
2. Click the gray ball, drag freely in any direction, then release. Pull down for an upward launch; the two bands pull toward their respective posts. Alternatively focus the canvas, hold Down to charge, use Left/Right to aim, and release Down. Pulls above the posts can now launch downward; drag direction is unrestricted.
3. Collect silver ($0.01), gold ($0.10), and red ($0.25) coins. Falling onto silver/gold/red platforms gives a spring response building toward 420/660/900 px/s upward rebound over 0.12 seconds and a $1/$5/$10 reward. Both tier targets and retained fall velocity have been reduced by 60%. Each platform pays once, shakes, and falls away.
4. Settle on the ground to receive a detailed payout and optional personal-best notification. Choose Visit Garage or Relaunch. All accepted runs receive at least $5 before adding platform rewards.
5. In the green terminal garage, choose a category and upgrade. Level squares show purchased, next, empty, and locked levels. Purchases immediately apply and save; limits, prerequisites, money, and scrap are validated.

New pilots have no jetpack. Once purchased, hold Space for upward thrust. The Starter Jetpack includes level 1 Left and Right Thrusters; use A/Left Arrow and D/Right Arrow, then purchase higher levels to improve steering. All sustained thrust consumes the same finite tank. Up Arrow activates a separately purchased emergency booster, once per key press, with charges replenished at the beginning of each run. Fuel does not regenerate except through purchased Landing Fuel Cells on a platform bounce.

Coin Magnet and Scrap Magnet attract their respective pickups. Radar shows available bounce platforms only, including off-screen platforms. Aerodynamics reduces drag. Cyan orbital scrap appears at 45,000 ft and is credited separately at run completion. Above that configurable threshold, oxygen drains; exhausted oxygen damages health, mitigated by Space Suit upgrades. Depleted health disables propulsion and platform rebounds so the player returns to the ground. Oxygen tanks extend available survival time. Oxygen refills on platform bounces with the Oxygen Recycler; health does not refill midflight. Both reset for the next run.

R or Reset Flight abandons the current run without payout while preserving permanent progress. Save Slots also abandons an unfinished run; purchases and completed flights remain saved. The garage can also be visited before launching. Desktop mouse/keyboard is the primary control scheme. Canvas menu controls have equivalent keyboard-accessible buttons, revealed when focused with Tab.

## Progression and persistence

Three localStorage slots contain pilot name, integer-cent wallet, spendable scrap, lifetime debris collected, all upgrade levels (including equipment unlocks), altitude and horizontal records, flight count, Earth tier, and the future Moon milestone. Purchases and completed runs autosave; slots are independent. The previous foundation's v1 wallet and launcher levels migrate into slot 1 if no v2 slots exist. Unfinished flights are not restored. Storage failures are shown in the interface; session play remains available.

Earth is fully implemented as the current tier. Moon at 500,000 miles (2,640,000,000 ft) is the current goal milestone; Moon and Mars environments, technology tiers, and orbital mechanics are deferred. The equipment/economy data can be extended with later destination upgrades.

## Configuration and architecture

- `src/config.ts`: physics, sky markers, generation density, coin/platform values, gameplay effects, survival, payout formula, and future destinations.
- `src/upgrade-definitions.ts`: garage categories, names, level limits, prerequisites, benefits, base prices, and price growth (1.8).
- `src/main.ts`, `game.ts`, `state.ts`: initialization, fixed-step loop, orchestration, and menu/aiming/launching/flying/crashing/results/garage states.
- `src/player.ts`, `slingshot.ts`, `camera.ts`, `input.ts`: simulation, swept platform/pickup contacts, launcher, camera transforms, and controls.
- `src/world.ts`: deterministic nearby chunks, tiered targets, collectibles, reward deduplication, attraction, and short-lived effects.
- `src/scoring.ts`, `economy.ts`, `saves.ts`: integer-cent payouts, data-driven purchases, records, and slot persistence.
- `src/flight-equipment.ts`: finite fuel, directional/upward thrust, booster charges, bounce fuel, oxygen, and health.
- `src/rendering.ts`, `atmosphere.ts`, `interface.ts`: world drawing, continuous sky, radar, Canvas HUD, menus, garage, and results.
- `src/style.css`, `index.html`: minimal browser shell and accessible controls.
- `tests/foundation.mjs`: bounded purchase, save, collision, currency, fuel, survival, and sky checks.

World x points right; y points up; ground is y=0. A world unit renders as one CSS pixel and displays as five feet. The simulation runs fixed 1/120-second steps driven by elapsed time; hidden tabs pause, and long frames are capped. Rendering remains separate from simulation. Sky colors sample world altitude, blending smoothly between markers and approaching black above deep space. Stars have deterministic world positions.

Chunks outside the nearby region are removed. Consumed object IDs remain in a run-level ledger, so revisiting regenerated chunks never farms a platform or pickup. Active world objects stay bounded to the nearby region; the ledger grows only with actual contacts during a run and clears on relaunch. Platforms use top-crossing collision detection and choose the earliest hit along a falling movement segment. Pickup checks also sweep the movement segment.

Base payout combines distance, maximum altitude, and coin value; altitude and airtime multipliers apply to that base. A minimum-run bonus then protects short flights, and platform rewards are added afterward at face value. Scrap is never converted into money.

Browser launch feel, balancing, UI layout, and end-to-end progression are left for manual playtesting. A passing build or focused check is not browser gameplay verification.

## Reactive propulsion

Thruster upgrades define target speeds rather than unlimited acceleration. Left and Right each start at 160 px/s, adding 30 px/s per additional level independently. Horizontal acceleration starts at 1,000 px/s², adding 150 px/s² per additional level. Upward acceleration starts at 750 px/s², adding 120 per Engine Power level.

The controller compares current velocity with the requested direction's target every physics step. It immediately accelerates toward the target, applies up to 2.2 times normal acceleration against opposing velocity, and reduces force as the target approaches. Gravity and drag compensation help hold the target. Fuel-limited acceleration cannot overshoot the target; releasing the key leaves momentum to normal flight physics. Faster slingshot, platform, or booster momentum is preserved, but sustained propulsion cannot increase it further in the same direction. Holding an active thruster consumes fuel even when its current velocity is already at or above the motor's target speed. Simultaneous left/right input cancels horizontal thrust.

Response speed, acceleration limits, reversal strength, and target speed increments are centralized in `src/config.ts`. Build and focused mechanics checks passed; browser responsiveness remains for manual testing.


## Free slingshot aiming

The pouch follows the grabbed point without horizontal, vertical, or radial position clamps. Pointer capture permits dragging outside the canvas, and release samples the final pointer position. The camera stays anchored during aiming to prevent the world from moving underneath the cursor; flight tracking resumes after release.

Each band calculates its own stretch beyond resting length and pulls toward its own post. The launcher configuration exposes separate left/right band stiffness and slack, plus post height offset, thickness, and launch efficiency. Both forces combine into the launch vector. Width upgrades change anchor geometry and lateral leverage; height upgrades change post geometry and spring-energy capacity; band upgrades increase stiffness and capacity. Launch speed scales linearly with pull distance: pulling halfway from the resting pouch to the ground gives 50% of the launcher tier capacity, and reaching the ground gives 100%. Longer and sideways drags remain unrestricted; speed stays capped at tier capacity. Near-rest or completely slack bands do not launch.

The Canvas bands use the same anchor positions as simulation, the dashed direction indicator follows the resulting launch vector, and the power readout shows resulting spring power rather than distance toward a drag limit. Below-ground upward releases preserve launch energy at ground contact. Browser aiming feel remains for manual testing.

## Current physics and return equipment

This update supersedes the earlier reactive target-speed controller. Sustained propulsion now applies constant upgrade-dependent force to the current velocity without a horizontal or vertical target-speed cap, artificial reversal multiplier, or automatic gravity compensation. Horizontal acceleration is 250 px/s² plus 150 per additional thruster level, reaching 1,600 px/s² at level 10 (the previous level-5 strength); upward acceleration is 330 px/s² plus 44 per Engine Power level. Opposite thrust naturally brakes and reverses momentum. Fuel limits the acceleration available in the final partial step; release stops adding force. Gravity, drag, booster impulses, and falling terminal behavior remain separate systems.

Gravity uses smooth interpolation with a 30% downward-gravity floor at 400,000 feet, followed by the destination attraction transition described below. The same altitude scale affects detached platforms. `src/physics.ts` owns this altitude function; thresholds and strength remain configured in `src/config.ts`.

Teleporter is an ADD-ONS purchase ($300 plus 3 spendable debris, unlocked after 8 lifetime debris). Press T during flight to use its one charge per run, including during a health-depleted return. It arrives at exactly 10,000 ft within 600 world units horizontally of the launcher, with randomized speed of 150–450 px/s and any direction. It preserves money, scrap, flight statistics, fuel, oxygen, and health. The camera moves to the destination and nearby chunks refresh. The jump adds no traveled distance and collects nothing along the teleport path. It does not refill equipment or revive the player.

Oxygen Recycler is a GEAR upgrade with five levels, starting at $100 and unlocked after 3 lifetime debris. Each fresh platform bounce restores 2%, 4%, 6%, 8%, or 10% of oxygen capacity at levels 1–5, capped at the tank's capacity. It works alongside Landing Fuel Cells; duplicate platform contacts cannot repeatedly restore resources.

Purple coins pay $5 before Coin Value Tier scaling. Purple platforms pay $20 before Platform Value Tier scaling and have a 1,800 px/s rebound target, twice red's 900 px/s. Both use the existing pickup/bounce effects and reward safeguards. Generation weights are silver 76%, gold 17%, red 4%, and purple 3%, so purple is slightly rarer than red. Weights, rewards, and bounce strength are configurable.

The HUD shows gravity percentage and remaining teleport charges. Existing save slots receive new upgrade fields at level zero. The build and focused mechanics checks pass; manual browser testing is still needed for flight feel and balance.
